How many fraudulent Social Security beneficiaries are there?

A Bayesian estimate from DOGE's flagged records and SSA's own books.

Bayesian inferencePublic dataInteractive

The claim

In early 2025, Elon Musk and the Department of Government Efficiency (DOGE) claimed that millions of dead people were marked as alive in Social Security's database — "tens of millions," Musk posted, "definitely dead" yet listed as alive — and announced that ~3.26 million numberholders aged 120+ had been reclassified as deceased in a records cleanup.

The question everyone argued past each other on: flagged records are not payments. How many beneficiaries are actually fraudulent — actually receiving money they shouldn't get?

The model

Let θ be the probability that a reported beneficiary's payments are fraudulent or improper. Prior θ ~ Beta(a, b), in three stances:

  • Skeptical (0.02%) — true fraud sits well below measured improper-payment rates, which are mostly errors.
  • Neutral (0.28%) — the blended OASDI stewardship improper-payment rate for FY2024, in dollar terms.
  • Alarmist (1.0%) — an order of magnitude above anything measured; the hidden-fraud thesis.

Then Nfraud = R · θ and Nactual = R − Nfraud, with R = 68,455,973 treated as a known admin count. Separately, the ghost check: π = P(a flagged record is actually paid) ~ Beta(44000, 18856000) from the OIG's measurement, so Npaid(F) = F · π for any flagged count F. Everything is conjugate, so every posterior below is closed-form — move the sliders and watch them update.

What the posteriors say

  • Even the alarmist prior — an order of magnitude above anything measured — puts fraudulent beneficiaries at ~685k [593k–782k]: under 1% of the rolls, ~$15B/year against ~$1.5T paid.
  • P(fraudulent > 1,000,000) is effectively zero under all three stances.
  • The ghost check is the starkest result: millions of flagged records convert to thousands of payments — 3.26M flagged → ~7,600 paid; even the 20M claim → ~46,600.
  • Remember the direction of the bias: stewardship rates measure improper payments (mostly errors), so these posteriors are upper bounds on fraud, not estimates of it.

Caveats

  • Improper ≠ fraud. SSA's books measure payment errors, not criminal fraud. Every number above overstates true fraud to the extent errors dominate.
  • The 44,000 figure comes via secondary reporting on the July 2023 OIG report; direct verification against the primary report is still pending.
  • DOGE’s 3.26M cleanup figure is via secondary reporting of a DOGE X announcement.
  • Prior strength (20k pseudo-observations default) is a judgment call — that's what the slider is for.
  • R is treated as a known admin count; in reality it has its own (small) reporting uncertainty.
